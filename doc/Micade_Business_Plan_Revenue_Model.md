# Micade Business Plan

## Section 3: Revenue Model

Status: Working draft
Phase: Phase 1 - Build the Company
Owner: Business Architecture Agent
Supporting agent: Enterprise Architecture Agent

## 3.1 Section Purpose

This section defines how Micade should earn revenue during the first service-led phase and how that revenue can expand into support, education, templates, automation, AI products, and SaaS.

The revenue model should stay practical, clear, and grounded in validated customer needs.

## 3.2 Approved Revenue Foundation

Approved starting business model:

- Service-led growth

Approved first customer segment:

- Local service businesses

Approved starter packages:

- Digital Presence Starter
- Growth System Setup
- AI and Automation Assist

## 3.3 Revenue Streams

Proposed revenue streams:

- One-time service projects
- Monthly support retainers
- Paid discovery or audits
- Workshops and practical training
- Templates and playbooks
- Automation setup packages
- AI workflow kits
- Future lightweight software tools
- Future SaaS products

Recommended starting revenue streams:

1. One-time service projects
2. Monthly support retainers
3. Paid discovery or audit services

Reason:

These streams are closest to the approved service-led growth model and can generate revenue before Micade invests in larger products.

## 3.4 Pricing Logic

Recommended pricing model:

- Hybrid pricing

Hybrid pricing means Micade should use fixed starting packages with custom scope adjustments when a project requires more work.

Why hybrid pricing fits Micade:

- Small businesses need clear starting prices.
- Service projects often vary by scope.
- Micade should avoid underpricing complex work.
- Clear packages make marketing easier.
- Custom adjustments protect delivery quality.

## 3.5 Starter Package Pricing Direction

Pricing should not be treated as final until market validation is complete.

Proposed pricing structure:

- Digital Presence Starter: entry-level one-time project
- Growth System Setup: mid-level one-time project
- AI and Automation Assist: focused setup project or add-on
- Monthly Support: optional recurring service after launch
- Paid Discovery or Audit: low-commitment entry offer

Pricing should account for:

- Discovery time
- Strategy and planning
- Content and messaging
- Design and development
- Automation setup
- AI workflow guidance
- Review and revision time
- Launch support
- Documentation
- Post-launch support

## 3.6 Monthly Support Model

Recommended decision:

Micade should offer monthly support from the beginning, but not force it into every package.

Proposed monthly support options:

- Basic Care: updates, monitoring, minor edits, and reporting
- Growth Support: content, analytics review, lead flow improvement, and recommendations
- Automation Support: workflow maintenance, AI usage support, and process improvement

Monthly support matters because it can create recurring revenue, long-term client relationships, better results, and continued learning.

## 3.7 Paid Discovery Model

Micade should consider a paid discovery or audit offer for businesses that are not ready for a full project.

Proposed discovery offer:

- Digital Growth Audit

Includes:

- Website and digital presence review
- Messaging review
- Lead capture review
- Local visibility review
- Workflow and automation opportunity review
- Practical recommendations
- Best-fit package recommendation

The audit can also become a lead generation tool if offered as a limited free or discounted entry offer during early validation.

## 3.8 Future Revenue Expansion

Future revenue should grow from validated service patterns:

- Templates from repeated project documents
- Workshops from repeated client training
- AI workflow kits from repeated automation patterns
- Software tools from repeated operational needs
- SaaS products from validated workflows
- Certifications from mature education and implementation systems

## 3.9 Revenue Risks

Key risks:

- Pricing too low for the amount of strategy and delivery required
- Offering too many pricing options too early
- Selling support without clear boundaries
- Customizing every project too much
- Building products before proving demand
- Promising revenue outcomes that Micade does not control

## 3.10 Revenue Controls

Recommended controls:

- Every package must define included and excluded work.
- Every proposal must define scope, timeline, deliverables, revision limits, and approval points.
- Custom work must be priced separately.
- Monthly support must define response time, included hours or task limits, and exclusions.
- Pricing should be reviewed after the first 5 to 10 discovery calls or client projects.

## 3.11 Decisions Captured

- Micade should begin revenue generation through one-time service projects.
- Monthly support should be available from the beginning as an optional recurring offer.
- Hybrid pricing is recommended: fixed starting packages with custom scope adjustments.
- Paid discovery or audits should be considered as an entry offer.
- Future revenue should grow from validated service patterns.

## 3.12 Why These Decisions Matter

This revenue model gives Micade a realistic way to start earning revenue while protecting quality.

It also creates a path from client services into recurring support, education, templates, AI products, automation kits, and SaaS without forcing product development too early.

## 3.13 Suggested Improvements

- Create package scope tables.
- Create a paid discovery/audit template.
- Define monthly support tiers and boundaries.
- Build a simple financial model after pricing is approved.
