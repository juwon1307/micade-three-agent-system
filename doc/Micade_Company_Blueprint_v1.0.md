Phase: Phase 1 - Build the Company
Primary owner: Micade leadership
Strategic review: Enterprise Architecture Agent

## Document Purpose

This document defines Micade's first working company blueprint.

It translates the approved brand direction into a practical company model covering audience, offers, delivery, operations, and growth.

Micade follows documentation before implementation. This blueprint should guide the Business Plan, Website Technical Specification, service delivery systems, content planning, product planning, and future hiring.

## Approved Company Decisions

- Micade is an integrated digital growth company.
- Micade combines technology, education, AI, software, and digital growth.
- The first priority audience is small businesses.
- The first approved launch segment is local service businesses.
- Micade starts with service-led growth.
- The first approved service packages are Digital Presence Starter, Growth System Setup, and AI and Automation Assist.
- The delivery model should be documented, repeatable, and quality-focused.
- Micade should grow from validated service delivery into education, templates, automation, AI products, SaaS, community, events, and certifications.

## Section 1: Company Foundation

### 1.1 Company Category

Micade is an integrated digital growth company.

Micade combines technology, education, AI, software, and digital growth to help small businesses learn, build, and grow.

### 1.2 Company Purpose

Micade exists to help small businesses become more capable, visible, efficient, and competitive in the digital economy.

The company should connect strategy, learning, implementation, automation, and growth support into one practical ecosystem.

### 1.3 First Priority Audience

Approved first priority audience:

- Small businesses

Approved first launch segment:

- Local service businesses

Local service businesses are a practical first segment because they often need clearer websites, stronger local visibility, better lead generation, workflow automation, customer communication systems, and simple AI adoption.

### 1.4 Initial Company Offers

Approved initial offer categories:

- Digital presence: websites, landing pages, business profiles, and brand messaging.
- Digital growth: lead generation systems, SEO basics, content strategy, analytics, and conversion improvement.
- Automation: workflow automation, customer follow-up systems, forms, CRM support, and task reduction.
- Practical AI adoption: AI tools, business process support, content workflows, and productivity systems.
- Education and training: practical lessons, workshops, templates, and guided implementation.
- Software solutions: lightweight internal tools, dashboards, portals, and future SaaS products.

### 1.5 Company Positioning

Approved positioning:

For small businesses that want to compete and grow in the digital economy, Micade provides integrated technology, education, AI, software, and digital growth solutions that turn ideas into practical outcomes.

### 1.6 Business Direction

Micade should begin with approved service-led growth for local service businesses and gradually expand into education, products, AI systems, SaaS, community, events, and certifications.

Recommended phased direction:

1. Start with high-value digital growth services for local service businesses.
2. Turn repeated delivery knowledge into templates, playbooks, and training.
3. Package common systems into reusable software and automation products.
4. Build scalable education and AI-assisted products.
5. Expand into broader small-business and organizational markets.

### 1.7 Operating Principles

Micade should operate by these principles:

- Documentation before implementation
- Excellence over speed
- Practical outcomes over hype
- User-centered design
- Ethical AI and responsible technology
- Measurable business value
- Security, accessibility, and performance by design
- Sustainable growth

### 1.8 Success Measures

Proposed early success measures:

- Number of small businesses served
- Quality of delivered websites, systems, and automations
- Improvement in client digital presence
- Lead generation or conversion improvements where measurable
- Client satisfaction and retention
- Reusable templates, playbooks, and internal systems created
- Education content produced from real delivery experience

## Section 2: Services and Offer Model

### 2.1 Offer Model Purpose

This section defines Micade's first practical service model.

The offer model should help Micade start with service-led growth for local service businesses while building reusable knowledge, templates, systems, and future product opportunities.

### 2.2 Initial Service Categories

Approved service categories:

- Digital presence
- Digital growth
- Automation
- Practical AI adoption
- Education and training
- Software solutions

These categories should work together as one integrated growth system, not as disconnected services.

### 2.3 First Three Paid Service Packages

Approved Package 1: Digital Presence Starter

Purpose:

Help a local service business establish a clear, trustworthy digital presence.

Includes:

- Website or landing page structure
- Core brand messaging
- Service descriptions
- Contact and lead capture setup
- Basic local SEO setup
- Mobile-friendly page structure
- Analytics setup recommendation

Best for:

Local businesses that need a professional online presence or need to improve an outdated website.

Approved Package 2: Growth System Setup

Purpose:

Help a local service business turn its digital presence into a simple lead generation and follow-up system.

Includes:

- Landing page or service page improvement
- Lead capture flow
- Customer inquiry form
- Follow-up workflow
- Basic CRM or spreadsheet-based tracking
- Content and offer recommendations
- Performance measurement plan

Best for:

Local businesses that already have some online presence but need better inquiries, follow-up, and growth structure.

Approved Package 3: AI and Automation Assist

Purpose:

Help a local service business save time and improve consistency with simple automation and practical AI workflows.

Includes:

- Workflow review
- Repetitive task identification
- AI-assisted content or customer communication workflow
- Automation setup recommendation
- Forms, notifications, or simple task-routing workflow
- Staff training notes
- Responsible AI usage guidance

Best for:

Local businesses that need operational support but are not ready for custom software or complex AI systems.

### 2.4 Delivery Model

Recommended delivery stages:

1. Discovery
2. Diagnosis
3. Proposal
4. Build
5. Review
6. Launch
7. Support
8. Learnings captured

Every project should produce reusable internal knowledge where possible.

### 2.5 Value Proposition

Micade helps local service businesses improve digital visibility, customer inquiries, workflow efficiency, AI readiness, and long-term growth through integrated services.

The value is not just a website or a tool. The value is a practical growth system that combines strategy, implementation, learning, and improvement.

### 2.6 Offer Principles

Micade offers should be:

- Easy to understand
- Business-outcome focused
- Practical to deliver
- Scalable into repeatable systems
- Suitable for small-business budgets
- Strong enough to build case studies
- Designed to create future templates, training, automation, and software products

### 2.7 What Micade Should Avoid

Micade should avoid:

- Offering too many services at launch
- Selling complex custom software before delivery systems are mature
- Promising guaranteed revenue or ranking outcomes
- Using unclear package names
- Taking projects with no measurable business problem
- Building one-off work that creates no learning or reusable process

## Section 3: Operating Model

### 3.1 Operating Model Purpose

This section defines how Micade should organize and deliver work during the first service-led phase.

The operating model should keep delivery practical, documented, repeatable, and scalable.

### 3.2 Core Operating Units

Proposed early operating units:

- Strategy and Client Discovery
- Brand and Messaging
- Website and Digital Presence
- Growth and Analytics
- Automation and AI Workflows
- Education and Client Enablement
- Documentation and Knowledge Management
- Operations and Quality Assurance

At the early stage, one person or small team may cover multiple units. The purpose is not to create bureaucracy; it is to make responsibilities clear.

### 3.3 Delivery Workflow

Approved delivery workflow:

1. Inquiry
2. Discovery
3. Diagnosis
4. Scope
5. Proposal
6. Build
7. Review
8. Launch
9. Support
10. Learnings captured

Every client project should produce internal learning that improves Micade's templates, service process, education content, and future product ideas.

### 3.4 Minimum Project Documents

Each client project should include:

- Client brief
- Problem statement
- Scope of work
- Deliverables list
- Timeline
- Approval checkpoints
- Launch checklist
- Support notes
- Lessons learned

### 3.5 Role Model

Proposed initial roles:

- Project Lead: owns client communication, scope, timeline, and delivery quality.
- Brand and Content Lead: owns messaging, page content, tone, and service descriptions.
- Technical Lead: owns website, automation, integrations, and technical quality.
- Growth Lead: owns SEO basics, lead capture, analytics, and performance recommendations.
- AI and Automation Lead: owns practical AI workflows, automation design, and responsible AI guidance.
- Documentation Lead: owns project records, playbooks, templates, and knowledge capture.

These roles can be combined at the beginning, but they should remain distinct as responsibilities.

### 3.6 Quality Standards

Micade delivery should be:

- Clear
- Professional
- Useful
- Accessible
- Secure
- Mobile-friendly
- Measurable
- Maintainable
- Documented

No client work should be considered complete without review against the approved brand, scope, technical quality, and launch checklist.

### 3.7 Client Communication Rhythm

Recommended communication rhythm:

- Discovery summary after the first meeting
- Scope confirmation before work begins
- Progress update at agreed milestones
- Review request before launch
- Launch confirmation after delivery
- Support and improvement notes after launch

Micade should communicate in a balanced voice: professional enough for businesses, simple enough for learners.

### 3.8 Internal Knowledge System

Micade should capture reusable knowledge from every project:

- Common client problems
- Useful service patterns
- Reusable website sections
- Automation templates
- AI workflow examples
- Content examples
- Objections and client questions
- Delivery risks
- Time estimates
- Results and lessons learned

This knowledge should become the foundation for future education products, templates, playbooks, software, and AI tools.

### 3.9 Governance Rules

Operating decisions should follow the Brand Guidelines governance model:

- Proposed
- Reviewed
- Approved
- Archived or replaced

Major changes to offers, positioning, delivery quality, pricing logic, or customer segment should be reviewed before being applied.

## Section 4: Growth Model

### 4.1 Growth Model Purpose

This section defines how Micade should grow from a focused service-led company into a broader ecosystem of education, AI, software, products, community, events, and certifications.

The growth model should keep Micade ambitious without allowing the company to expand too quickly or lose operational focus.

### 4.2 Growth Principle

Micade should grow from real delivery experience.

Services should create:

- Client insight
- Case studies
- Reusable templates
- Training material
- Automation patterns
- Product ideas
- Software requirements
- AI workflow knowledge

This prevents Micade from building products or courses without proof of real market needs.

### 4.3 Growth Phases

Phase 1: Service Foundation

Focus:

- Serve local service businesses.
- Validate the first three service packages.
- Build delivery systems, templates, checklists, and case studies.
- Capture repeatable problems and solutions.

Main outcome:

Micade becomes capable of delivering clear digital growth outcomes consistently.

Phase 2: Education and Templates

Focus:

- Turn repeated service knowledge into training content.
- Create practical guides, workshops, templates, checklists, and playbooks.
- Support small businesses that need learning before or alongside implementation.

Main outcome:

Micade begins scaling knowledge beyond one-to-one service delivery.

Phase 3: Automation and AI Products

Focus:

- Package repeated AI and automation workflows.
- Build simple tools, prompt systems, workflow kits, and AI-assisted business resources.
- Help small businesses save time and improve consistency.

Main outcome:

Micade creates productized value from repeated service patterns.

Phase 4: SaaS and Software Products

Focus:

- Build software around validated business problems.
- Prioritize lightweight tools, dashboards, portals, CRM helpers, automation hubs, and growth tracking systems.
- Use service and education experience to shape product requirements.

Main outcome:

Micade expands from services and education into scalable software revenue.

Phase 5: Community, Events, and Certifications

Focus:

- Build a learning and growth community around small-business digital transformation.
- Host workshops, practical events, and cohort-based programs.
- Create certifications around Micade-approved skills, tools, and workflows.

Main outcome:

Micade becomes an ecosystem, not just a service provider.

### 4.4 Growth Channels

Proposed growth channels:

- Client referrals
- Local business outreach
- Educational content
- SEO and GEO content
- Case studies
- Workshops
- Partnerships
- Templates and free resources
- Email list
- Social proof from delivered work

### 4.5 Productization Path

Micade should productize only after patterns repeat.

Productization sequence:

1. Deliver manually.
2. Document the process.
3. Repeat with multiple clients.
4. Identify what is reusable.
5. Create a template or checklist.
6. Turn the template into training.
7. Automate part of the process.
8. Build software only when the workflow is validated.

### 4.6 Growth Metrics

Proposed growth metrics:

- Number of qualified leads
- Number of discovery calls
- Proposal acceptance rate
- Number of completed projects
- Client satisfaction
- Client retention
- Monthly recurring support revenue
- Number of reusable templates created
- Number of case studies created
- Education content output
- Repeatable automation workflows identified
- Product ideas validated by real client need

### 4.7 Expansion Rules

Micade should expand only when:

- Current services can be delivered consistently.
- Delivery quality is documented and repeatable.
- There is evidence of repeated customer need.
- The team can support the new offer without damaging current delivery.
- The new offer supports Micade's brand and business architecture.
- The opportunity can produce measurable value.

Micade should avoid:

- Launching too many offers at once.
- Building SaaS before validating workflows.
- Creating courses disconnected from real business problems.
- Overinvesting in brand visuals before business delivery is clear.
- Expanding into broad enterprise work before small-business delivery is stable.

### 4.8 Strategic Growth Map

Recommended growth order:

1. Local service business services
2. Client templates and delivery playbooks
3. Practical education content
4. AI and automation workflow kits
5. Lightweight software tools
6. SaaS products
7. Community and events
8. Certifications and partner ecosystem

## Open Approval Items

The following items should be reviewed before this blueprint is treated as fully approved:

- Choose the first two or three local service niches for outreach.
- Decide whether education is bundled into services from the beginning or launched later as a separate offer.
- Decide whether monthly support revenue is part of the first offer model.
- Decide whether package pricing should be fixed, custom, or hybrid.
- Approve or revise the proposed operating units.
- Choose the first tool or system for project tracking and knowledge management.
- Approve or revise the phased growth model.
- Decide whether education or automation products should come first after service delivery is stable.
