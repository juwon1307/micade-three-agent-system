# Micade Business Plan

## Section 1: Executive Summary

Status: Working draft
Phase: Phase 1 - Build the Company
Owner: Business Architecture Agent
Supporting agent: Enterprise Architecture Agent

## 1.1 Business Summary

Micade is an integrated digital growth company that helps small businesses learn, build, and grow through technology, AI, software, education, and digital growth solutions.

Micade will begin with service-led growth for local service businesses, then expand into education, templates, automation products, AI products, SaaS, community, events, and certifications.

## 1.2 Starting Market Focus

Approved first priority audience:

- Small businesses

Approved first launch segment:

- Local service businesses

Proposed early customer profile:

- Businesses that depend on local visibility, trust, inquiries, repeat customers, and operational consistency.
- Businesses that need a better website, clearer messaging, stronger lead capture, simple automation, or practical AI support.

## 1.3 Initial Offers

Approved starter packages:

- Digital Presence Starter
- Growth System Setup
- AI and Automation Assist

These packages allow Micade to solve immediate business problems while building reusable knowledge for future products and training.

## 1.4 Business Model

Approved starting model:

- Service-led growth

Proposed revenue streams:

- One-time service projects
- Monthly support retainers
- Workshops and training
- Templates and playbooks
- Automation setup packages
- Future AI products
- Future SaaS products

## 1.5 Growth Strategy

Micade should grow in this order:

1. Local service business services
2. Client templates and delivery playbooks
3. Practical education content
4. AI and automation workflow kits
5. Lightweight software tools
6. SaaS products
7. Community and events
8. Certifications and partner ecosystem

## 1.6 Competitive Direction

Micade should not compete as only a web design agency, only a training platform, only an AI company, or only a software vendor.

Micade's advantage should come from integrating strategy, implementation, education, AI, automation, software thinking, and growth support into one practical system for small businesses.

## 1.7 Early Success Measures

Proposed early success measures:

- Qualified leads generated
- Discovery calls completed
- Proposals sent
- Proposal acceptance rate
- Projects completed
- Client satisfaction
- Monthly support clients
- Case studies created
- Reusable templates created
- Education assets created
- Repeatable automation workflows identified

## 1.8 Key Risks

Proposed early risks:

- Offering too many services too early
- Taking unclear projects with weak business outcomes
- Underpricing service work
- Building products before validating repeatable client needs
- Creating education content without real delivery insight
- Weak project documentation
- Inconsistent quality control

## 1.9 Decisions Captured

- Micade's Business Plan starts from the approved Brand Guidelines and Company Blueprint.
- The business starts with local service business services.
- The first approved packages are Digital Presence Starter, Growth System Setup, and AI and Automation Assist.
- Revenue should begin with services and may expand into support, education, templates, AI products, and SaaS.
- The plan should avoid invented financial claims until pricing and market assumptions are reviewed.

## 1.10 Why These Decisions Matter

These decisions keep the business plan realistic.

They give Micade a practical path to revenue, client learning, case studies, and future product validation before investing heavily in larger products.

## 1.11 Suggested Improvements

- Choose the first two or three local service niches.
- Define starter package pricing.
- Decide whether monthly support should be sold from the beginning.
- Define first 90-day execution targets.
- Create a simple financial model after pricing is approved.

## 1.12 Important Questions For Approval

1. Which two or three local service niches should Micade target first?
2. Should monthly support retainers be included from the beginning?
3. Should pricing be fixed, custom, or hybrid?
4. What should be Micade's first 90-day business target?
