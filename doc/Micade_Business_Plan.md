# Micade Business Plan

Status: Consolidated working draft
Phase: Phase 1 - Build the Company
Primary owner: Micade leadership
Strategic review: Enterprise Architecture Agent
Business support: Business Architecture Agent
Documentation support: Writer Agent

## Document Purpose

This document defines Micade's first working business plan.

It uses the approved Micade Brand Guidelines and Company Blueprint to define the starting market focus, revenue model, execution roadmap, risks, and early validation path.

This plan avoids invented financial claims. Exact pricing, revenue projections, and market claims should be added only after customer discovery, pricing validation, or explicit owner approval.

## Approved Business Decisions

- Micade is an integrated digital growth company.
- The first priority audience is small businesses.
- The first approved launch segment is local service businesses.
- Micade starts with service-led growth.
- The first approved starter packages are Digital Presence Starter, Growth System Setup, and AI and Automation Assist.
- The recommended pricing direction is hybrid pricing: fixed starting packages with custom scope adjustments.
- Monthly support should be considered from the beginning as an optional recurring offer.
- Growth should move from services into templates, education, automation, AI products, SaaS, community, events, and certifications.

## Section 1: Executive Summary

### 1.1 Business Summary

Micade is an integrated digital growth company that helps small businesses learn, build, and grow through technology, AI, software, education, and digital growth solutions.

Micade will begin with service-led growth for local service businesses, then expand into education, templates, automation products, AI products, SaaS, community, events, and certifications.

### 1.2 Starting Market Focus

Approved first priority audience:

- Small businesses

Approved first launch segment:

- Local service businesses

Proposed early customer profile:

- Businesses that depend on local visibility, trust, inquiries, repeat customers, and operational consistency.
- Businesses that need a better website, clearer messaging, stronger lead capture, simple automation, or practical AI support.

### 1.3 Initial Offers

Approved starter packages:

- Digital Presence Starter
- Growth System Setup
- AI and Automation Assist

These packages allow Micade to solve immediate business problems while building reusable knowledge for future products and training.

### 1.4 Business Model

Approved starting model:

- Service-led growth

Proposed revenue streams:

- One-time service projects
- Monthly support retainers
- Workshops and training
- Templates and playbooks
- Automation setup packages
- Future AI products
- Future SaaS products

### 1.5 Growth Strategy

Micade should grow in this order:

1. Local service business services
2. Client templates and delivery playbooks
3. Practical education content
4. AI and automation workflow kits
5. Lightweight software tools
6. SaaS products
7. Community and events
8. Certifications and partner ecosystem

### 1.6 Competitive Direction

Micade should not compete as only a web design agency, only a training platform, only an AI company, or only a software vendor.

Micade's advantage should come from integrating strategy, implementation, education, AI, automation, software thinking, and growth support into one practical system for small businesses.

## Section 2: Market and Customer Strategy

### 2.1 Approved Market Focus

Approved first priority audience:

- Small businesses

Approved first launch segment:

- Local service businesses

Approved starting business model:

- Service-led growth

### 2.2 Proposed First Niches

Recommended first local service niches:

1. Home and property services
2. Health, wellness, and beauty services
3. Professional local services

Reason:

These businesses often depend on trust, local visibility, customer inquiries, bookings, follow-up, reputation, and repeat business. Those needs align well with Micade's first three packages.

### 2.3 Customer Problems

Micade should focus on small businesses with practical digital growth problems:

- Weak or outdated website
- Poor local visibility
- Unclear service messaging
- Low trust from online presence
- Inconsistent lead capture
- Slow customer follow-up
- Manual repetitive tasks
- No simple customer tracking system
- Limited understanding of AI tools
- No clear content or growth strategy
- Difficulty turning inquiries into booked work

### 2.4 Customer Buying Triggers

Proposed buying triggers:

- The business is losing leads because its website or online presence is weak.
- The owner is tired of handling repetitive customer communication manually.
- The business wants more inquiries but lacks a simple lead capture system.
- The business has tried posting online but has no clear content or conversion strategy.
- The business owner is curious about AI but does not know how to apply it safely.
- The business needs a professional digital presence before advertising or expansion.

### 2.5 Ideal Early Customer Profile

Proposed ideal early customer:

- Local service business with a clear service offer.
- Owner or manager can make buying decisions quickly.
- Business has real customer demand but weak digital systems.
- Business values professionalism and practical growth.
- Business is willing to improve operations, not just buy a website.
- Business can provide content, approvals, and feedback during delivery.

Avoid early customers who:

- Have no clear service offer.
- Want guaranteed results without operational cooperation.
- Need complex enterprise software immediately.
- Have no budget for professional work.
- Cannot review or approve project materials on time.
- Want only cheap one-off design with no growth objective.

### 2.6 Outreach Strategy

Proposed first outreach channels:

- Direct local business outreach
- Referrals from existing relationships
- Simple educational content
- Local SEO content
- Case-study-driven marketing
- Partnerships with complementary service providers
- Free checklist or audit offer

Recommended first outreach message:

Micade helps local service businesses improve their digital presence, capture more qualified inquiries, automate simple workflows, and use practical AI with confidence.

### 2.7 Customer Journey

Proposed customer journey:

1. Awareness: customer sees Micade content, referral, outreach, or resource.
2. Interest: customer identifies a digital growth problem.
3. Discovery: Micade reviews the business need.
4. Diagnosis: Micade defines the gap and best-fit package.
5. Proposal: customer receives scope, deliverables, timeline, and next steps.
6. Delivery: Micade builds the agreed solution.
7. Launch: solution goes live or workflow is activated.
8. Support: customer receives improvement guidance.
9. Expansion: customer may buy support, training, automation, or additional growth services.

### 2.8 Market Validation Plan

Micade should validate the market before making strong claims.

Recommended validation actions:

- Interview 10 local service business owners.
- Review 20 local business websites and digital presence gaps.
- Test one simple audit or checklist offer.
- Run discovery calls before finalizing pricing.
- Track objections, common needs, and package fit.
- Convert repeated problems into service templates.

## Section 3: Revenue Model

### 3.1 Revenue Streams

Proposed revenue streams:

- One-time service projects
- Monthly support retainers
- Paid discovery or audits
- Workshops and practical training
- Templates and playbooks
- Automation setup packages
- AI workflow kits
- Future lightweight software tools
- Future SaaS products

Recommended starting revenue streams:

1. One-time service projects
2. Monthly support retainers
3. Paid discovery or audit services

### 3.2 Pricing Logic

Recommended pricing model:

- Hybrid pricing

Hybrid pricing means Micade should use fixed starting packages with custom scope adjustments when a project requires more work.

### 3.3 Starter Package Pricing Direction

Pricing should not be treated as final until market validation is complete.

Proposed pricing structure:

- Digital Presence Starter: entry-level one-time project
- Growth System Setup: mid-level one-time project
- AI and Automation Assist: focused setup project or add-on
- Monthly Support: optional recurring service after launch
- Paid Discovery or Audit: low-commitment entry offer

Pricing should account for discovery, strategy, content, design, development, automation, AI workflow guidance, review, launch support, documentation, and post-launch support.

### 3.4 Monthly Support Model

Recommended decision:

Micade should offer monthly support from the beginning, but not force it into every package.

Proposed monthly support options:

- Basic Care: updates, monitoring, minor edits, and reporting
- Growth Support: content, analytics review, lead flow improvement, and recommendations
- Automation Support: workflow maintenance, AI usage support, and process improvement

### 3.5 Paid Discovery Model

Micade should consider a paid discovery or audit offer for businesses that are not ready for a full project.

Proposed discovery offer:

- Digital Growth Audit

Includes:

- Website and digital presence review
- Messaging review
- Lead capture review
- Local visibility review
- Workflow and automation opportunity review
- Practical recommendations
- Best-fit package recommendation

### 3.6 Revenue Controls

Recommended controls:

- Every package must define included and excluded work.
- Every proposal must define scope, timeline, deliverables, revision limits, and approval points.
- Custom work must be priced separately.
- Monthly support must define response time, included hours or task limits, and exclusions.
- Pricing should be reviewed after the first 5 to 10 discovery calls or client projects.

## Section 4: Execution Roadmap

### 4.1 First 90-Day Roadmap

Month 1: Foundation and Validation

Focus:

- Finalize Brand Guidelines, Company Blueprint, and Business Plan.
- Choose first local service niches.
- Create discovery questionnaire.
- Create digital growth audit checklist.
- Create proposal template.
- Build simple lead tracker.
- Identify first 30 outreach targets.
- Run early customer interviews and discovery calls.

Month 2: Outreach and First Projects

Focus:

- Begin direct outreach, referrals, and educational content.
- Test Digital Growth Audit as an entry offer.
- Refine packages based on customer feedback.
- Close first service projects where possible.
- Document objections, questions, and common customer problems.
- Create first project delivery checklists.

Month 3: Delivery and Repeatability

Focus:

- Deliver first projects with strong documentation.
- Capture lessons learned from each project.
- Improve package scopes and support boundaries.
- Create reusable templates from delivery.
- Define monthly support offer.
- Prepare first case study format.
- Decide next priority: education asset or automation kit.

### 4.2 First 90-Day Targets

Proposed targets:

- Complete 3 core Phase 1 documents: Brand Guidelines, Company Blueprint, and Business Plan.
- Choose 2 or 3 local service niches.
- Build 1 discovery questionnaire.
- Build 1 digital growth audit checklist.
- Build 1 proposal template.
- Build 1 lead tracker.
- Identify 30 outreach targets.
- Complete 10 customer interviews or discovery conversations.
- Send 5 qualified proposals.
- Complete or begin 1 to 3 starter projects.
- Create 1 reusable delivery checklist.
- Create 1 case study template.

These are proposed planning targets, not guaranteed business outcomes.

### 4.3 Execution Priorities

- Finish the business foundation.
- Validate the customer segment.
- Package the service offers.
- Build delivery assets.
- Start controlled outreach.

### 4.4 Key Performance Indicators

Proposed early KPIs:

- Outreach targets identified
- Discovery conversations completed
- Customer problems documented
- Proposals sent
- Proposal acceptance rate
- Starter projects started
- Starter projects completed
- Client satisfaction
- Reusable templates created
- Monthly support interest
- Repeatable automation opportunities identified
- Case studies created

### 4.5 Risk Management

Key execution risks:

- Moving to website design before business decisions are clear
- Choosing too many customer niches
- Pricing before understanding customer willingness to pay
- Offering unclear package scope
- Failing to document delivery lessons
- Taking projects that do not match Micade's direction
- Selling AI or automation without proper boundaries

Risk controls:

- Keep the Phase 1 document sequence intact.
- Limit first outreach to 2 or 3 niches.
- Use discovery calls before final pricing.
- Define package inclusions and exclusions.
- Capture lessons learned from every client conversation and project.
- Review all public claims against brand governance.
